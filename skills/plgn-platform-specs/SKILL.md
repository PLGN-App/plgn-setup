# Platform limits and habits

Guidance for the platforms plgn schedules to. Use it to write posts that fit
before they reach the server.

**Commands must copy the numbers a writer needs into that writer's prompt.**
Agents cannot read this file.

## The table

| Platform | Limit | Aim for | Opening | Hashtags | Links |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | 3,000 | 900–1,300 | First 2 lines before "see more" — a claim or a number | 3–5, at the end | Pushed down in the post; put one in the first comment or accept less reach |
| **X** | 280 | 180–240 | The whole post is the opening; put the surprise first | 0–2, only if natural | Take about 23 characters however long they are |
| **Instagram** | 2,200 | 300–600 | First line only; the rest hides behind "more" | 5–10, at the end or first comment | Not clickable in captions — say "link in bio" or leave it out |
| **Facebook** | 63,206 | 100–250 | Short beats long; the platform buries walls of text | 0–3 | Clickable, with a preview |
| **TikTok** | 2,200 | 80–150 | The caption supports the video, it never replaces it | 3–5, inline | Not clickable |

**Aim for the target, not the limit.** Write to the target so the server's check
has room. A post at 2,995 of LinkedIn's 3,000 fails the moment anyone edits a
word.

## Carousels

Not every platform takes one, and the ones that do cap it differently.

| Platform | Carousel | Max frames |
|---|---|---|
| **Instagram** | yes | up to 10 |
| **LinkedIn** | yes | up to 10 |
| **Facebook** | yes | up to 10 |
| **X** | yes | up to 4 |
| **TikTok** | no — video only | — |

A carousel caption is written differently from a single picture's, because
the first frame already does the work a caption normally opens with. The
frame itself carries the hook the reader sees before they swipe, so the
caption doesn't have to earn attention a second time — it can move straight
to what the caption is actually for on that platform: the hashtags, the
context, the call to swipe on, or, on LinkedIn, the argument the frames only
sketched.

## Rewrite per platform, never copy

The same idea must be **written again** for each platform, never pasted. A
LinkedIn post pasted into X is a cut-off LinkedIn post, and readers can tell.

Worked example — one idea, three platforms:

> **The idea:** teams waste the first week of every quarter deciding what to work
> on, because last quarter's review never happened.

**LinkedIn** — an argument with evidence and room to develop:

> Most teams lose the first week of every quarter.
>
> Not to holidays. To deciding what to do.
>
> The pattern is always the same: the quarter ends, nobody runs the review, and
> the next planning meeting starts from memory instead of records. So the first
> week goes on reconstructing what happened — badly.
>
> The fix takes 90 minutes. Run the review *before* the quarter closes, while
> people still remember. Write down what shipped, what slipped, and why. Then
> planning starts from a document instead of an argument.
>
> The teams that do this don't plan better. They just start sooner.

**X** — one turn, cut down to the surprise:

> Most teams lose week 1 of every quarter to deciding what to work on.
>
> Not because planning is hard. Because nobody ran last quarter's review, so
> planning starts from memory.
>
> 90 minutes before the quarter closes buys back a week.

**Instagram** — the first line carries everything, the rest is scannable:

> You didn't lose week one to holidays.
>
> You lost it rebuilding a quarter nobody wrote down.
>
> Run the review before the quarter ends — while people still remember why
> things slipped. 90 minutes, and planning starts from a document instead of an
> argument.
>
> Save this for the last week of the quarter 📌

Notice what changed: not just the length. The *shape* of the argument changed —
LinkedIn gets how it works, X gets the punchline, Instagram gets the reframe and
a reason to save it.

## Openings that work

- **A specific number.** "90 minutes" beats "a short meeting".
- **A correction.** Name the assumed cause, then the real one.
- **A cost.** Say what the reader is already losing, then the fix.

Avoid: questions as openers ("Ever wonder why...?"), "In today's fast-paced
world", and any opening that could start any post on any subject.

## The server decides

These limits are guidance for writing. plgn enforces the real ones on the
server, and a brand's own settings may be stricter than this table. **Where they
disagree, the server wins** — follow the **plgn-gate-recovery** skill rather than
arguing with the error.
