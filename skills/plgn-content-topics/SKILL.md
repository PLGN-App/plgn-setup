# Content topics

A topic is a **repeated argument**, not a category. "Educational" is a category.
"Migration stories" is an argument: old tools trap teams, and we have the
receipts.

Categories tell a writer nothing. Arguments tell them what to say next.

A topic here is the same thing plgn saves in the workspace — it has a name, a
description, and posts attached to it. What you decide in this skill becomes a
real record the user can see and reuse.

## Never produce these

> ❌ Educational · Promotional · Engaging · Behind-the-scenes · Industry news

They are formats dressed up as strategy. They fit every brand in every market,
which is exactly what makes them useless.

**The test: could this topic belong to a competitor unchanged?** If yes, it is
not a topic.

> ❌ "Educational" — anyone's
> ✅ "Migration stories" — a brand whose buyers are stuck on old tools and whose
> proof is migration case studies

## How many

Three to five.

- **Fewer than three** and the feed repeats itself within a month.
- **More than five** and no topic gets enough posts to build a pattern — and the
  pattern is the whole point. A reader should be able to guess roughly what this
  brand posts about, and be right.

Prefer three strong topics over five where two are padding. Always say why you
chose the number you chose.

## Each post must fit one topic only

Hand a reader ten of the brand's posts and they must file each under **exactly
one**, with no ties.

If two topics would both plausibly claim the same post, they are one topic
written twice. Merge them and find a real second — or accept that the brand has
fewer topics than hoped, which is honest and fixable.

Check before committing: for each topic, name a post that belongs to it and
would be wrong under every other one.

## Healthy proportions

Across a month, aim for roughly:

| Share | Kind | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ~50% | Points the audience gains from whether or not they buy | Earns the right to the rest |
| ~30% | Proof — cases, results, how it works, demonstrations | Converts the readers the first group brought in |
| ~20% | Direct offers, launches, asks | The reason the account exists |

These are proportions, not quotas. A launch month reasonably flips them; what
fails is a feed that is *permanently* flipped, where every post asks for
something and none gives anything.

## The best topics come from gaps

The strongest topic is usually an argument the brand's competitors all avoid or
miss. Where everyone in a market says the same three things, the fourth thing is
free — and being first beats being better.

When a topic comes from a gap, write that down. It tells a writer why the topic
matters and stops it drifting back toward the category everyone else occupies.

## Spotting a topic that has run out

A topic has run out when it keeps saying the same thing instead of moving on.
Signs:

- **The same kind of opening again and again.** Five posts opening with a number
  is one post published five times.
- **Posts you could swap.** Exchange two and nothing is lost.
- **The argument stopped moving.** Each post repeats the point; none takes it
  somewhere new — a harder case, an objection, a consequence.
- **Reaching for unrelated subjects** to fill slots, because the topic itself
  has nothing left.

**The fix is not more posts.** Either narrow it into a sharper argument it can
actually develop, or retire it and promote one that came from a gap. Retiring a
topic is a normal outcome, not a failure — topics have a life, usually a few
months of regular posting.

## Topics with almost nothing in them

The opposite problem: a topic that exists in the plan and gets almost no posts.
Either it is not really a topic, or the writer finds it hard — often because it
needs proof the brand does not have yet.

Say which. "This needs two customer results before it can carry posts" is
something someone can act on; quietly under-serving it is not.

## Talking about this to the user

Use the word **topic**, not "pillar". Never say a topic is "starved" or
"exhausted" — say it has too few posts, or that it repeats itself. See the
**plgn-reply-style** skill.
